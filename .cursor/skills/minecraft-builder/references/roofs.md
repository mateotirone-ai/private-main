## Why roofs decide the silhouette

Roofs are the first read from distance. Wrong pitch/style makes even good walls
look unfinished. Match roof family to program; break large roofs into sections.

## Universal detailing rules

- Overhang **1–2 blocks** past walls on every side (often 2).
- Upside-down stairs under eaves as trim.
- Ridge often **slabs**, not a full-block spine.
- Gable rake / rim: upright stair facing out + upside-down stair behind
  (stepped profile).
- Contrasting edge material vs fill; mix stairs + slabs of related blocks for
  texture noise.
- Chimney: stone footing, campfire in throat for smoke.
- Light attic/upper volumes; plan interior headroom with pitch.

## Style → use

| Style | When | Build notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gable | Cottages, small houses, barns | Stairs step to ridge; odd width helps center |
| Cross-gable | L/T footprints | Build primary gable first; secondary meets in valleys |
| Hip | Villas, gazebos, finished-from-all-sides | Slopes on 4 sides; careful corners |
| Flat + parapet | Modern / desert modern | Slabs + wall/trapdoor/iron railing; terrace optional |
| Shed / mono-pitch | Industrial, lean-tos, workshops | Single slope; good for work-form buildings |
| Mansard | Extra upper volume, formal | Steep lower + shallow/flat upper |
| Gambrel | Barns / warehouses | Steep lower, shallow upper |
| Thatched look | Rustic cottages | Hay / kelp / warm wool stepped thick; dark ridge trim |
| Cone / tower | Turrets | Decreasing rings; odd diameters 7/9 center cleanly |
| Dormers | Break long slopes | Short pillars; dormer roof lower than main ridge |

## Material guidance

- Spruce / dark oak stairs: rustic & warm roofs (default-friendly here)
- Mud bricks: project roof family
- Terracotta (red/orange/brown): tile-like Mediterranean
- Copper (waxed stages): patina accents / special roofs
- Deepslate tiles / stone brick: heavy civic or fortress (use sparingly vs world palette)
- Concrete / quartz / smooth stone: modern flats
- Living roofs: moss, dirt planters, shallow parapets

## Construction workflow that reduces pain

1. Odd-numbered primary footprint when possible.
2. Frame eaves/overhang all around first.
3. Build **main** roof volume lengthwise; ignore wings until main reads.
4. Frame secondary roofs; fill; delete conflicting stairs at joins.
5. If stuck with even center: sideways half-triangle of slabs can fake a ridge.
6. Add dormers only after main slopes work.
7. Dress: chimney, rake trim, lightning rod/banners only if they serve silhouette.

## Mistakes

- Flat plank lid on a pitched-architecture house
- Roof flush with walls (no overhang)
- Same block as walls for entire roof
- Over-steep wasting interior / under-steep looking unfinished
- Dormer taller than main roof
- Ignoring valleys where roofs meet
- Giant chimney on a tiny cottage (proportion fail)
